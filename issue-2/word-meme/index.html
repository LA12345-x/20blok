‘I Love That for You’

On paper, it reads like support. In practice, it’s often distance.

“I love that for you” 💬 turned into being socially supportive while emotionally checked out.

It can mean “good for you, but I’d never touch that,” 😅 or “I don’t get it, but I won’t argue.” 🤷‍♂️

“Support” has never sounded more non-committal.

The elephant in the room

Are we…

a) cheering for each other

b) politely opting out, or

c) cutting down someone’s moment just to lift our own

Dive deeper: the origin story of the phrase, here on Instagram.

Authenticity is the new performance art.

Because sometimes “support” is just the softest possible no. 🚫✨
